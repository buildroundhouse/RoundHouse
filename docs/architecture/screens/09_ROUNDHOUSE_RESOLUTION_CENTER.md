# ROUNDHOUSE — RESOLUTION CENTER

## Purpose

The Resolution Center is Roundhouse's system for keeping responsibility visible until an issue, question, request, or required action is actually finished.

It is not a generic task list and it is not a notification feed.

A Resolution creates a persistent responsibility object around an Entity and the people involved:

**Question / request → responsibility → response → action → verification → resolution → permanent history**

The goal is simple:

**The toggle stays alive until the creator is satisfied that the matter has actually been resolved.**

---

# Position in the Command Center

The Resolution Center is opened from the **far-left control on the bottom Command Center bar**.

The bottom control uses the recognizable Resolution toggle symbol. Tapping the bottom-bar Resolution control opens the Resolution Center as a destination/workspace.

The Resolution Center is separate from the right-side working tabs. Daily Grind, Tasks / Lists, Receipts, and Properties remain right-side working overlays. Resolution is a bottom-bar destination because it is a larger responsibility and closeout system rather than a temporary working sheet.

The Resolution system can also surface relevant items inside Daily Grind when they matter to the current day, but Daily Grind does not own or duplicate the Resolution.

---

# Resolution Center List

The Resolution Center is ordered by responsibility and urgency.

The primary order is:

1. **Red — Needs Your Attention**
2. **Green — Waiting on Them**
3. **Gray — Resolved**

All active Red items appear above Green items because Red means the current user owes the next response or action.

Resolved items fall below the active items and become gray.

A recently resolved item that a participant has not yet opened remains prominent at the top of the Resolved group with a marker such as:

**RESOLVED — NEW**

or

**RESOLVED — UNREAD**

After that participant opens the Resolution and sees how it ended, the New/Unread marker clears and the item falls naturally into the chronological resolved history.

This gives Roundhouse a way to prompt someone to read the final outcome without creating a separate reminder or notification system.

---

# Physical Resolution Control

Each Resolution is represented by a distinctive physical control.

## Pill-Shaped Base Placard

The base is a horizontal **pill-shaped placard**.

It should be mostly flat and only lightly dimensional — not a heavy 3-D switch panel.

The base placard carries the escalation state. The gray toggle hardware itself does not change color.

### Escalation Around the Base

Normal active state has no escalation color around the placard.

When repeated unanswered follow-up accumulates, the **rim around the pill-shaped base** escalates:

- first unanswered follow-up: **Yellow rim**
- second unanswered follow-up: **Red rim**
- third unanswered follow-up: **animated fire around the base**

The fire should appear to engulf or animate around the pill base without turning the toggle hardware itself into a colored object.

The escalation treatment answers:

**How much unanswered pressure has accumulated around this Resolution?**

It is separate from the toggle position, which answers whose turn it is.

## Cylindrical Toggle

On top of the pill-shaped base is the Resolution toggle.

The switch is a **round cylindrical control with a flat collar/cap on top**.

The toggle remains gray/neutral in every state. It does not become green, red, yellow, or gray based on responsibility.

Its physical position carries the responsibility state.

### Left Position — Green Indicator

When responsibility is on the other person — meaning the current user has answered, acted, or is waiting for the other side — the toggle rests to the **left**.

A small flat **green indicator at the left end of the placard** identifies the state.

Meaning:

**Waiting on them.**

### Right Position — Red Indicator

When the current user owes the next answer or action, the toggle moves to the **right**.

A small flat **red indicator at the right end of the placard** identifies the state.

Meaning:

**Needs your attention.**

The toggle itself remains gray. Only the small end indicator communicates Red or Green responsibility.

### Resolved State

When the creator closes the Resolution, the active responsibility state disappears and the item becomes gray in the Resolved section.

The placard no longer carries active Yellow/Red/Fire escalation.

Resolved is a historical state, not another side of the responsibility toggle.

---

# Identity Around the Toggle

Every Resolution must visibly show where it came from and who created it.

**Above the toggle:** the Property, Business, or other Entity context in which the Resolution was created.

**Below the toggle:** the author/creator who created the Resolution.

The creator identity matters because **only the creator has authority to close that Resolution**.

The system should therefore make the creator unmistakable rather than hiding that information inside details.

---

# Tapping the Toggle

The visible toggle is **not switched off directly by tapping the switch**.

Tapping the Resolution control opens the Resolution itself.

The opened Resolution shows the dialogue and history that developed around it:

- the original question, request, statement, or issue;
- who created it;
- the Entity context;
- responses from the participants;
- responsibility changes;
- linked actions or proof of completion;
- the final closeout.

The toggle is therefore both a visual responsibility indicator and the doorway into the actual Resolution.

---

# Relationship to Messages

A Resolution may be created from a Message conversation.

When that happens, the Resolution keeps a durable link to the conversation that caused it to exist.

Inside the Resolution, the person can use an action such as:

**View Original Conversation**

This takes them back to the relevant Messages thread so they can review what was originally said without duplicating the entire private conversation into the Resolution record.

The Resolution may contain its own focused dialogue around resolving the matter while the originating Message thread remains its own communication record.

---

# Responsibility Passing Back and Forth

The Resolution toggle moves according to **whose turn it is**, not according to who originally created the Resolution.

If Person A asks Person B a question, responsibility moves to Person B.

If Person B answers but asks Person A a new question or makes a statement requiring Person A's decision/action, responsibility moves back to Person A.

A Resolution may therefore move left and right several times before it is actually resolved.

The creator retains the sole authority to close it, but does not necessarily retain the current responsibility throughout the conversation.

---

# Answering Is Not Always Resolving

A response does not automatically close the Resolution.

Example:

The creator asks:

**Can you schedule this for Saturday?**

The other person replies:

**Yes, I will schedule it for Saturday.**

That answer can satisfy the immediate question and move responsibility, but it does not necessarily resolve the matter. The requested action was not merely to promise scheduling — it was to actually schedule the work.

When the Calendar action occurs, the Resolution can receive the linked result:

**Scheduled — Saturday, 9:00 AM**

The creator can then review that action. If it satisfies the original Resolution, the creator closes it.

This is a governing distinction:

**A reply can move responsibility. Only completion of the required outcome allows the creator to resolve the matter.**

---

# Linked Actions

A Resolution should be able to receive evidence that a required Roundhouse action has actually occurred.

Examples include:

- an appointment was scheduled in Calendar;
- a requested document was added;
- a photograph was supplied;
- a decision was recorded;
- a requested follow-up was completed;
- another relevant Roundhouse Record was created.

The Resolution does not need to duplicate these systems. It should link to or display the relevant completion result so the creator can verify whether the requested outcome has been satisfied.

This is especially important when a conversation alone is not enough to prove completion.

---

# Closing a Resolution

Only the Resolution's creator can close it.

The creator opens the Resolution, reviews the final response and any required linked action, and deliberately marks the matter resolved.

Closing does not erase the Resolution.

It moves into the gray Resolved history as a permanent record of:

- what was raised;
- who was responsible at each stage;
- what was said;
- what action occurred;
- who verified the outcome;
- when the creator closed it.

Participants who have not yet seen the final outcome receive the **Resolved — New/Unread** treatment until they open it.

---

# Resolution Center Governing Rules

1. **Resolution is the far-left bottom Command Center control.**
2. Tapping the bottom Resolution control opens the Resolution Center.
3. Each Resolution is represented by a lightly dimensional pill-shaped base placard with a gray cylindrical toggle and flat top collar.
4. The toggle hardware itself does not change color.
5. **Left + Green end indicator = waiting on the other person.**
6. **Right + Red end indicator = the current user owes the next response/action.**
7. Escalation belongs to the rim of the pill-shaped base: **Yellow → Red → animated Fire** as unanswered follow-up accumulates.
8. Responsibility state and escalation state are two different signals and must remain visually separate.
9. The Entity/Property context appears above the toggle.
10. The Resolution creator appears below the toggle.
11. **Only the creator can close the Resolution.**
12. Tapping the visible toggle opens the Resolution; it does not directly turn the Resolution off.
13. New questions, statements, or required actions can pass responsibility back and forth between participants.
14. A response does not necessarily equal completion.
15. Required actions such as scheduling can feed their completed result back into the Resolution for verification.
16. A Resolution created from Messages retains a path back to the originating conversation.
17. Closing a Resolution does not erase it; it becomes gray permanent Resolution history.
18. A resolved item remains marked **New/Unread** for participants who have not yet opened the final outcome.
19. Red active items sort first, Green waiting items below them, and Gray resolved items below the active group.
20. **The goal of every Resolution is to make responsibility visible until the creator can verify that the matter is actually finished.**
