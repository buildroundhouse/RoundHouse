# ROUNDHOUSE — INVITATIONS

## Purpose

Invitations are the doorway for bringing a person into Roundhouse and for establishing legitimate participation in a Property, Commercial Facility, or Business.

Roundhouse does not create person-to-person social connections.

**An invitation either brings someone into Roundhouse, brings them into an Entity, requests access to an Entity, or establishes a Business-managed participant relationship.**

The invitation system must always preserve the distinction between:

- **Person** — the human;
- **Acting Identity / Role** — how the person participates;
- **Entity** — the Property, Commercial Facility, or Business they are participating through;
- **Authority** — who is allowed to approve or manage that participation;
- **Permission source** — why the person currently has access.

Titles and authority follow **`../ROUNDHOUSE_ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md`**. Shared interaction requires at least one paid party: the participating person or the person who owns or controls the Property or Business. Subscription eligibility, participant consent, and authorization are checked separately. An invitation remains pending until the required conditions are satisfied; a new account enters the Command Center only after its first completed relationship.

---

# 1. Invitation Center

Roundhouse has one dedicated **Invitation Center**.

Its primary Profile doorway appears immediately **before Find a Trade Professional**.

The Invitation Center is also opened by the prominent **Share Roundhouse** action near the Profile identity / splash area. These are two doorways into the same system, not two different invitation systems.

The Invitation Center contains:

- **Invite / Add Someone**;
- **Requests to You**;
- **Invitations You Sent**;
- **Pending Entity approvals**;
- **Share Roundhouse**;
- status such as Pending, Accepted, Declined, Cancelled, Expired, or Needs Setup;
- resend / cancel where appropriate;
- the Entity, intended Role, authority source, and requester / inviter where relevant.

The screen should make it obvious whether the item is:

- an invitation into Roundhouse;
- an invitation into a Business;
- an invitation into a Property / Facility;
- an access request;
- a Business requesting or exercising Property participant authority.

---

# 2. Profile Placement

On the editable/private Profile, the outward-looking section begins in this order:

1. **Invite / Share Roundhouse**
2. **Find a Trade Professional**
3. **Residential Home Search**
4. **Commercial Facility Search**
5. **Discover**

The Invite row/card is intentionally before Find because inviting someone is a common relationship-building action and should not require searching the public ecosystem first.

Tapping the Invite row opens the Invitation Center.

The prominent Share Roundhouse action near the Profile identity area opens the same Invitation Center, focused on **Share Roundhouse**.

---

# 3. Share Roundhouse

**Share Roundhouse** is the acquisition / onboarding side of the invitation system.

It can invite someone who is not yet a Roundhouse user by phone, email, share sheet, or supported contact method.

A Share invitation can carry context such as:

- inviter;
- intended Entity;
- intended Role;
- intended Business relationship;
- pending Property / Facility participation;
- setup requirements.

The recipient may create or sign into their Roundhouse account and then continue the invitation rather than becoming a context-free new user.

Where Roundhouse awards Points for successful sharing, that reward belongs to the **Share Roundhouse** action and successful activation, not merely to sending a large number of invitations.

Share should remain personal rather than spammy.

---

# 4. Viewer

A Viewer invitation names the Residential Property or Commercial Facility, the information shared, and the permitted messaging scope. The Residential display title is **Home (Viewer)**.

The Viewer may be paid or unpaid. The Viewer or the person owning or controlling the Property must have a paid subscription for this shared relationship. Consent and authorization are required in addition to subscription eligibility.

**History Viewer** is the role inside the person's private History. That History is available only to its associated person after their first completed Property or Business connection. Share RoundHouse from History invites someone to the platform or a legitimate Property/Business connection, not into the sender's private History.

Both Viewer contexts provide Share RoundHouse and search/connect/setup paths. New Property or Business operations use the relationship established in that destination. Free accounts may create one Property and one Business. Ownership transfer starts when the legitimate claimant finds the record and requests transfer from its current controller.

---

# 5. Add-to-Entity Flow

Selecting a person does not by itself define the relationship.

The flow is:

**Select person → Where are you adding them? → How are they participating here? → Review authority / access → Send invitation**

Examples:

### Property / Facility

The invitation identifies the Property, the person's intended Property sub-role, and authorized scope. Residential choices use **Home Pro (Manager)**, **Home Pro (Teammate)**, **Home (Viewer)**, or another applicable approved Home title. An invited Trade or Supplier participant retains the appropriate Business role and receives separately authorized Property access.

### Business

The invitation uses an approved Trade or Supplier sub-role, with employment or subcontracting recorded as the working relationship. Owner, Manager, Lead, and combined positions use the exact parentheses and en dash defined in Roles & Authority.

The exact choices depend on the inviter's authority, the intended relationship, and subscription eligibility.

RoundHouse shows a plain-language confirmation before sending, for example:

**Invite Carlos Hernandez to DMT DESIGN BUILD as Trade Professional for subcontracted work?**

**Invite Maria Lopez to Spring Lake Residence as Home (Viewer)?**

---

# 6. Business → Subcontractor / Outside Trade Professional

When a Trade Professional or Trade Business wants to use a subcontractor, the subcontractor is first invited into the **Business relationship**.

The governing flow is:

**Business invites Sub → Sub accepts Business relationship → Business assigns / proposes Sub to Property → Property permission is resolved**

The subcontractor should not be told to independently find the homeowner's Property and request access.

The homeowner should not be forced to search for the subcontractor or manually reconstruct the Business's staffing decision.

The Business is responsible for bringing the participant forward because the Business is managing the job.

---

# 7. Claimed Property — Homeowner Approval

When the Property has a claimed Homeowner and the Trade Business does **not** have delegated participant-management authority, assigning a subcontractor to that Property creates a simple Homeowner approval request.

Example:

**JD Design Studios wants to add Carlos Hernandez to Spring Lake Residence for the kitchen project.**

**Approve | Decline**

The request identifies:

- Business;
- person being added;
- intended Role / working relationship;
- Property;
- Work / job context where applicable;
- requested permission scope;
- person who initiated the request.

The Homeowner does not need to locate or invite the subcontractor themselves.

---

# 8. Delegated Manager Authority — Automatic Property Approval

A Homeowner may explicitly give a Trade Professional or Business **Manager authority** that includes permission to manage Property participants.

When that authority is active, the authorized Manager may approve an appropriate subcontractor / participant into the Property on the Homeowner's behalf.

The flow becomes:

**Business invites Sub → Sub joins Business → Manager assigns Sub to Property → Property access is approved under delegated Manager authority**

The Homeowner does not receive another approval task.

Instead, the Homeowner receives an informational notification such as:

**JD Design Studios added Carlos Hernandez to Spring Lake Residence.**  
**Authorized through JD's Manager authority.**

The permanent record must preserve:

- who added the participant;
- which Business relationship was used;
- which Manager authority allowed the action;
- what permission scope was granted;
- when it happened.

Removing or narrowing Manager authority stops future automatic approvals outside the remaining scope.

---

# 9. Unclaimed Property

When a legitimate Trade Business is managing an **Unclaimed Property**, the Business may add the participants necessary to perform and document the work within its legitimate operational authority.

The Business should not be forced through a fake loop in which:

1. the Business creates or manages the Property;
2. its subcontractor requests access from the same Property;
3. the Business then approves the request it effectively caused.

Instead:

**Business manages Unclaimed Property → Business assigns authorized participant → appropriate Property access is created directly with the Business as the permission source.**

When the legitimate owner later claims the Property, Roundhouse preserves existing history and reevaluates current permissions under the claimed-owner governance model.

---

# 10. Permission Source

Every active participant permission should be able to answer:

**Why does this person have access?**

Useful permission-source categories include:

- Property Owner direct authorization;
- delegated Property Manager authorization;
- Business-derived assignment;
- authorized Facility management;
- direct independent Property relationship;
- Viewer invitation;
- other explicit governing authorization.

This source matters because permissions may end when the relationship that created them ends.

---

# 11. Business-Derived Property Access

If a subcontractor's Property access exists only because they are participating through a Trade Business, that access is **Business-derived**.

When the Business removes that subcontractor from the Business relationship, or otherwise ends the relationship that supplied the Property permission, Roundhouse automatically removes the subcontractor's **Business-derived current Property access**.

The Homeowner should not have to clean up the Business's former worker manually.

This does not erase:

- legitimate completed Work;
- Timeline history;
- CAPTURE evidence;
- messages / Resolutions the person legitimately participated in;
- attribution;
- their own legitimate work history.

Current access ends. Historical truth remains.

---

# 12. Independent Reconnection

A Business-derived relationship does not permanently bind the Homeowner and subcontractor to the original Trade Business.

If the original Business relationship ends and the Homeowner independently wants to continue working with that subcontractor, the Homeowner and Trade Professional may establish a **new direct Property relationship** through Roundhouse.

That new authorization has its own permission source.

Therefore:

**Business-derived Property access** ends with the governing Business relationship.  
**Independent Property access** survives because the Homeowner separately authorized it.

Historical records should clearly preserve which relationship applied at the time of each Work Record.

---

# 13. Acceptance

There is no silent person-to-Entity membership unless the person has already accepted the governing upstream relationship and an authorized Manager is exercising explicitly delegated Property authority within scope.

Examples:

- Joining a Business requires the invited person's acceptance.
- Becoming a Home Pro (Teammate) requires the invited person's acceptance.
- Becoming a Viewer requires the invited person's acceptance.
- A subcontractor who already joined a Business may receive Business-derived Property access when the Property Owner or delegated Manager approves the placement.

Acceptance and authority are separate questions:

**Did the person agree to participate?**  
**Did the Entity's authorized controller approve their access?**

Roundhouse resolves both without unnecessary duplicate steps.

---

# 14. Notifications and Push

Meaningful invitation events create Notification Center entries and are eligible for phone push.

Examples:

- **Sarah Miller invited you to Spring Lake Residence as a Viewer.**
- **JD Design Studios invited you to join its Trade Team.**
- **JD Design Studios wants to add Carlos Hernandez to your Property.**
- **Your request to join Canyon Ridge Facility was approved.**
- **Carlos Hernandez accepted your Business invitation.**
- **JD Design Studios added Carlos Hernandez under delegated Manager authority.**

Push taps deep-link to the Invitation Center, approval item, Entity, or relevant participant record.

Notifications provide awareness. The underlying Entity membership / permission record remains authoritative.

---

# 15. Pending Invitations

Pending invitations remain visible until accepted, declined, cancelled, expired, or otherwise resolved.

Pending people must not appear as active participants before the relationship is active.

The sender may see appropriate controls such as:

- resend;
- cancel;
- view intended Entity / Role;
- view whether setup is required;
- view whether Homeowner approval is pending.

The recipient may see:

- inviter;
- Entity;
- intended Role / relationship;
- relevant permission scope;
- Accept / Decline.

---

# 16. Requests for Access

Requesting access is the inverse of an invitation.

A person may request access only through a legitimate Roundhouse pathway and only for an Acting Identity they control.

The Entity's authorized Owner / Manager decides the request unless delegated authority establishes another valid decision path.

The request does not create temporary access while pending.

Requests and invitations use the same underlying participation lifecycle rather than creating parallel relationship systems.

---

# 17. Data / Logic Requirements

The canonical participation record should preserve at minimum:

- Entity ID;
- Person ID;
- Acting Identity / current Role;
- status;
- direction: invite or request;
- inviter / requester;
- authority source;
- permission source;
- Business source when access is Business-derived;
- delegated Manager authority reference when applicable;
- permission scope;
- created time;
- accepted / declined time;
- removal time;
- historical attribution.

A Property participation may therefore be direct or derived.

Roundhouse must be able to revoke only the permissions dependent on a relationship without deleting independent permissions the person may also hold.

Example:

Carlos may have:

- Business-derived access through JD Design Studios; and later
- an independent Homeowner-authorized relationship.

Ending the JD relationship removes the first source without destroying the second.

---

# 18. Role Titles and Invitation Language

Role categories and sub-role titles come from **`../ROUNDHOUSE_ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md`**. Invitations preserve the exact displayed title, the authority requested, and its source.

Use **Invite**, **Add / Invite**, and **Request Access** for the action. **Subcontractor** and other working descriptions accompany the role where useful. **Business-derived access** and **Independent Property access** identify permission sources.

---

## Governing Rule

**Invitations create legitimate participation through Entities. Profile exposes one Invitation Center before Find, and every Share Roundhouse doorway enters that same system. A Business brings its own subcontractors forward instead of sending them to negotiate access separately. Claimed Properties require Owner approval unless delegated Manager authority explicitly allows the Business to approve participants on the Owner's behalf; Unclaimed Properties allow the legitimate managing Business to establish necessary operational access. Business-derived Property permissions automatically end when their governing Business relationship ends, while history remains and independently authorized relationships survive. Property Viewer invitations establish scoped access to the named Property. History stays private to its associated person. Shared interaction requires at least one paid party and appropriate authorization.**
