# Roundhouse Property History & Records

## Purpose

**Property History / Records** is the durable archival view inside the Property Vault.

Its home is:

**Property Entity → Vault → Property History / Records**

It answers:

**What happened at this Property, and what durable Record proves it?**

Property History is not a second Timeline and it is not a duplicate archive of Work, CAPTURE, Maintenance, Standards, Assets, Documents, or Estimates / Invoices.

It is a retrieval view over the same underlying Records those systems create.

---

# 1. Timeline vs Property History

This distinction is governing.

## Property Timeline

The Property Timeline is the living chronological view of the Property.

It answers:

**What is happening here?**

It favors relevant chronological activity and the current shared story of the Property.

## Property History / Records

Property History is the durable archival retrieval view.

It answers:

**What happened here?**

It is designed for finding significant past work, maintenance, inspections, installations, ownership / participant changes, Standards evidence, Property changes, Asset lifecycle events, and other legitimate historical Records years later.

The same underlying Record may appear in both views without being duplicated.

---

# 2. History Screen

At the top:

**Property History**  
Property name beneath it  
**Search History**

A compact filter row may include:

**All | Work | Maintenance | Standards | Installations | Inspections | Property Changes**

Additional filters can be introduced when they solve real retrieval needs, but the screen should remain simple enough to scan.

The History view should feel like a clean archival Timeline: chronological, calm, and information-dense without looking like an active job board.

---

# 3. History Cards

A History card summarizes one meaningful Record or historical event.

A card may show:

- date / time
- event title
- short outcome
- related Asset / area
- people / Business involved when useful
- linked Record types
- Record status when relevant
- evidence indicator when useful

Example:

**May 14 — Annual HVAC Service**  
Trane XR15 · DMT DESIGN BUILD  
CAPTURE completed · service record available

Example:

**March 4 — Water Heater Replaced**  
New Asset created · warranty added · invoice linked

Example:

**Jan. 12 — Foundation Inspection**  
Engineer report added  
No corrective work required

Example:

**Sept. 8 — Pool Chemistry Standard Met**  
Photo evidence recorded

The card is a summary. Tapping it opens the underlying Record or a unified Record Detail view rather than creating a separate historical copy.

---

# 4. What Belongs in Property History

Property History should preserve meaningful durable events such as:

## Work

- completed repairs
- completed projects
- verified Work
- significant service calls
- corrective Work resulting from Standards drift

## Maintenance

- completed recurring maintenance
- major service intervals
- inspections tied to ongoing Property care
- deferred / skipped maintenance where historically meaningful

## Standards

- Standard evidence
- meaningful drift events
- corrective action connected to drift
- restoration to expected condition

## Installations & Asset Lifecycle

- Asset installation
- replacement
- major repair
- retirement
- significant component replacement
- warranty service

## Inspections & Reports

- home / specialty inspection
- engineering inspection
- permit inspection
- water / soil / environmental report
- significant findings and related Records

## Property Changes

- major remodel or alteration
- meaningful specification change
- significant system upgrade
- structural / site change
- major condition change

## Property Participation / Authority Changes

Where appropriate to the permanent Property record:

- ownership transfer
- significant authority change
- important participant lifecycle event

These events should not expose private membership information beyond the current person's legitimate permissions.

## Disclaimers and Resolutions of Condition

- accepted materially relevant Disclaimer
- later Record that resolves or supersedes the condition
- status transition from active concern to historical record

---

# 5. What Does Not Need to Clutter History

Not every system event deserves a prominent History card.

Examples that normally remain out of the main archival view unless materially relevant:

- routine UI actions
- search activity
- ordinary notification events
- trivial metadata edits
- every intermediate Work status change
- every background cadence calculation
- duplicate links to the same event

The underlying legitimate Record can remain preserved without every technical event becoming a visible History milestone.

---

# 6. Record Detail

Tapping a History item opens its underlying Record or a unified **Record Detail** presentation.

Record Detail may show:

- title
- date / time
- Property
- people / Business legitimately involved
- Record type
- outcome / summary
- related Asset
- related Work
- related Maintenance / Standard
- related CAPTURE session
- linked Documents
- related Estimate / Invoice
- photographs / evidence
- Record status
- corrections / disputes where applicable

The goal is to expose the relationships around one real Record rather than forcing the user to hunt across unrelated screens.

---

# 7. One Record, Multiple Views

Roundhouse should not create separate Records for every place an event needs to appear.

Example:

A water-heater replacement may be visible in:

- Property Timeline
- Property History
- Asset History
- Work
- CAPTURE results
- Vault Documents
- Estimate / Invoice history

These are different views and relationships around the same legitimate event and Records.

**Visibility in multiple places does not mean data duplication.**

---

# 8. Search History

**Search History** searches the archival Records available to the current person at the current Property.

Search can match useful terms such as:

- work title
- trade / category
- Asset name
- Business / provider
- room / Property area
- Standard
- document / inspection name
- material / component
- year / date context

Examples:

`water heater`  
`foundation`  
`HVAC 2025`  
`pool chemistry`  
`kitchen remodel`

Search filters the History view; it does not grant additional access.

---

# 9. Record Status

Where the governing Record model uses status, Property History preserves it.

A Record may be:

**Accepted · Not Accepted · Rejected · Disputed**

Status affects presentation but does not erase the Record.

A disputed or rejected Record should remain historically discoverable to people authorized to see it, with its status clearly visible.

---

# 10. Corrections, Superseding Records, and Disassociation

History must support truthful correction without rewriting the past.

A Record may be:

- corrected;
- disputed;
- superseded by a later Record;
- disassociated from the Property if the original Property connection was incorrect.

Where a Record is superseded, the relationship should be visible.

Example:

**Foundation concern documented — Jan. 12**  
Superseded / resolved by: **Engineer clearance — Feb. 2**

Disassociation removes an incorrect Property relationship without erasing legitimate contributor history elsewhere.

---

# 11. Assets and Asset History

Property History and Asset History complement each other.

**Property History** asks:

**What happened at this Property?**

**Asset History** asks:

**What happened to this particular piece of equipment?**

An HVAC service Record can appear in both because it is both a Property event and an Asset event.

There remains one underlying Record.

---

# 12. Documents and History

Documents answers:

**Where is the file?**

Property History answers:

**What happened?**

Example:

**History event:** Foundation inspected Jan. 12.  
**Document:** engineer's report.  
**Work:** corrective repair, if any.  
**Resolution:** unresolved decision or dependency, if one existed.

These remain connected without becoming duplicate records.

---

# 13. Maintenance and Standards

Maintenance and Standards create durable historical value when something actually happens.

Examples include:

- recurring maintenance completed;
- Standard met with evidence;
- Standard drift detected;
- corrective Work completed;
- inspection outcome recorded.

Routine future cadence calculations remain in Maintenance and do not need to appear as History events until they produce a legitimate occurrence or Record.

---

# 14. CAPTURE

CAPTURE provides real-time evidence of work actually performed.

When a CAPTURE session creates durable Property history, Property History references that same work session / Record.

Useful historical evidence may include:

- starting condition
- completion photographs
- materials / parts used
- service notes
- task completion
- check-in / check-out context

Property History should summarize the event and allow the user to drill into CAPTURE evidence when authorized.

---

# 15. Estimates / Invoices

Financial records remain governed by Estimates / Invoices.

Property History may show a useful relationship such as:

**Water Heater Replacement**  
Completed March 4  
Invoice #118 · Paid

History does not become an accounting ledger.

---

# 16. Ownership and Long-Term Property Continuity

A Property's legitimate history should survive changes in who owns, manages, services, or works at the Property.

Changing:

- Homeowner
- Trade Professional
- Trade Team Member
- Business relationship
- service provider
- other participant

does not erase legitimate Property history.

Access changes according to Role, authority, privacy, and visibility rules.

The underlying legitimate Property Record remains.

---

# 17. Permissions and Privacy

Property History is permission-aware.

A person may see only the historical Records they are legitimately authorized to see.

Examples:

- Homeowners may see broad Property history according to authority and visibility.
- Trade Professionals may need relevant past work, Asset, inspection, Standard, and service information necessary for authorized work.
- Trade Team Members receive only history appropriate to their legitimate work and permissions.
- Viewers remain view-only.
- private Business/internal records remain private unless deliberately shared.

Historical importance does not override privacy.

---

# 18. Property Handoff

Handoff may surface selected historical Records that help a newly authorized person understand the Property.

Examples:

- recent major work
- important Asset replacement
- significant inspection
- materially relevant prior condition
- current / recently resolved Standard drift

Handoff links to the underlying History / Record and does not create a handoff copy.

---

# 19. Legacy Integration

Useful legacy systems should feed the modern History architecture rather than survive as separate competing screens.

Relevant legacy sources include:

- work logs;
- completed Work Orders;
- recurring maintenance occurrences;
- Standards evidence history;
- ratings / verification where tied to legitimate completed work;
- Property member / ownership events where appropriate;
- Property notes deliberately promoted into durable Records;
- Asset service events.

Legacy UI does not govern the new experience. The underlying historical value should be preserved and mapped into the unified Record model.

---

## Governing Relationship

**Property Timeline = what is happening here.**  
**Property History = what happened here.**  
**Documents = where is the file.**  
**Asset History = what happened to this equipment.**

---

## Governing Rule

**Property History is the durable archival view of the Property's legitimate Records. It preserves what happened without creating a second copy of Work, CAPTURE, Maintenance, Standards, Assets, Documents, or financial records.**
