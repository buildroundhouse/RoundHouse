# ROUNDHOUSE — SOURCE OF TRUTH

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental architecture, language, and core value of Roundhouse. It is the governing reference for what Roundhouse is and how its primary objects relate.

Detailed rules for ownership, permissions, records, pathways, gamification, subscriptions, and advertising belong in their respective governing documents.

## Core Value

Roundhouse is a grassroots platform built to serve local communities.

At its center is the relationship between the **Homeowner** and the **Trade Professional** and the work they do together.

Roundhouse is designed to encourage good communication, proper documentation, accountability, and best business practices between the people caring for a property and the people performing the work.

For the **Homeowner**, Roundhouse provides a permanent record of the home—its work, repairs, improvements, materials, photos, decisions, and history.

For the **Trade Professional**, that same work builds a lasting portfolio record of the work they actually performed and contributed over time.

The Property record and the Trade Professional's work history are connected by the work while remaining independent records that can survive changes in ownership, employment, businesses, and relationships.

Roundhouse is intended to strengthen local relationships between homeowners, trades, workers, suppliers, and businesses serving the communities where they live and work.

## 1. People and Entities

Roundhouse has **People and Entities**.

A **Person** is a real human with one permanent personal account.

An **Entity** is a persistent place or organization where people participate and where activity and history belong.

The primary Entities are:

- **Property** — residential or commercial.
- **Business** — a company or operating organization.

**People do not connect directly to people. People connect through entities.**

## 2. Acting Identity

A Person can participate in Roundhouse in different capacities.

Before creating operational activity, Roundhouse must know:

**Who is acting, and within what Entity?**

The operating relationship is:

**Person → Acting Identity → Entity → Record**

Authentication identifies the human. It does not determine their operational role, authority, or authorship.

A bare authenticated user does not create operational records.

## 3. Roles

Roundhouse has eight base roles:

1. **Homeowner**
2. **Home Team**
3. **Trade Professional**
4. **Trade Team**
5. **Commercial Management**
6. **Commercial Team**
7. **Collaborator**
8. **Supplier**

A role describes the person's relationship to an Entity. It does not by itself establish ownership, leadership, or paid membership.

### Pro Designations

**Pro is a membership designation, not a base role.**

When applicable, the public-facing designation changes:

- Homeowner → **Home Pro**
- Home Team → **Home Team Pro**
- Trade Professional → **Trade Pro**
- Trade Team → **Trade Pro**
- Commercial Management → **Commercial Pro**
- Commercial Team → **Commercial Team Pro**

Collaborator and Supplier remain their base designations unless a separate Pro designation is established for them.

### Authority

**Owner** and **Lead** are authority designations separate from Pro membership.

Examples include:

- **Home Pro (Owner)**
- **Home Pro (Lead)**
- **Home Team Pro (Lead)**
- **Trade Pro (Owner)**
- **Trade Pro (Lead)**
- **Trade Pro**

An Owner does not automatically have to be the Lead. A Lead does not have to be the Owner.

## 4. Property

A Property exists independently of the person who owns it, creates its Roundhouse profile, manages it, or performs work there.

A provider may create a Property profile when the homeowner is not yet using Roundhouse. Creating that profile does not make the provider the real-world owner.

Ownership, stewardship, access, and participation may change while the Property and its history remain.

There should be **one Roundhouse Property for one real-world property**.

## 5. Business

A Business is an Entity with its own persistent identity, participants, history, records, and relationships.

The people who own, manage, or work for a Business may change without creating a new Business Entity or erasing its history.

A Business is not simply a person's profile operating under a company name.

## 6. Relationships

People participate in Entities through roles and relationships.

Relationships determine what someone may do within an Entity.

Relationships can be requested, granted, changed, transferred, or removed without changing the underlying identity of the Person or Entity.

**Collaborator** is a neutral relationship. It allows someone to be given access to an Entity without making that person part of the Home, Trade, or Commercial team.

## 7. Records and Attribution

Operational activity belongs to an Entity and remains attributable to the Person and Acting Identity that created it.

This includes work, photos, notes, tasks, materials, receipts, approvals, communications, and other Timeline activity.

A Record does not lose its original authorship because ownership, employment, membership, or access later changes.

The same underlying Record may appear in the appropriate Property, Business, and personal history without becoming separate conflicting copies.

## 8. Ownership, Control and Creation

These are separate concepts:

**Real-world ownership · Roundhouse control/stewardship · access · leadership · authorship · record authority**

Creating an Entity does not automatically establish real-world ownership.

Roundhouse must allow ownership, control, leadership, and participation to change without destroying the Entity or its existing history.

Detailed rules governing these relationships belong in **ROUNDHOUSE — Ownership & Record Authority**.

## 9. Roundhouse History

Roundhouse preserves what happened over time: who participated, what work occurred, what was documented, and in what capacity each person was acting.

Current roles, relationships, ownership, leadership, permissions, and membership may change.

**Historical attribution remains.**

## Governing Rule

New Roundhouse features should not violate the **Person → Acting Identity → Entity → Record** architecture or confuse role, membership, ownership, leadership, access, and authorship with one another.
