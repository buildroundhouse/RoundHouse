# ROUNDHOUSE — OWNERSHIP & RECORD AUTHORITY

## Purpose

This document defines ownership, control, claims, record authority, disputes, and historical preservation for Roundhouse Entities.

**Creation, ownership, control, access, and record authority are separate.**

## 1. Entities

Roundhouse has two primary types of Entities:

**Property Entities**
- Residential
- Commercial

**Business Entities**
- Trade
- Supplier

There should be **one Roundhouse Entity for each real-world Property or Business**.

Owners and participants may change. The Entity and its legitimate history remain.

## 2. Creating an Entity

Roundhouse searches for an existing Entity before another is created.

A person with a legitimate reason to interact with a Property or Business may create its Roundhouse Entity if it does not already exist.

Creating an Entity does not require the creator to own the real-world Property or Business.

### Creation capacity

When a brand-new Property or Business Entity is created, the creator enters in **one of only two creation capacities**:

**Owner** — the creator is asserting that they legitimately own the real-world Property or Business.

**Temporary Admin** — the creator is not asserting ownership. They are establishing and administering the Roundhouse Entity for a legitimate operational reason until the legitimate Owner claims it or ownership is otherwise established.

**Owner and Temporary Admin are creation / authority states. They are not substitutes for the person's normal Role or relationship to the Entity.**

A new Entity is never created by selecting an unrelated participation Role merely to gain setup access.

### Temporary Admin and unclaimed Entities

An Entity created by a Temporary Admin is marked **Unclaimed**.

Temporary Admin exists so Roundhouse can document real work even when the legitimate Property or Business Owner has not joined Roundhouse yet.

Examples include:

- a Trade Professional creating a subcontractor's Business so that subcontractor work can be documented;
- a Trade participant creating a Residential or Commercial Property in order to document legitimate work there;
- Commercial Management creating a Commercial Property that has not yet been claimed by the legal Owner;
- another legitimate participant establishing the Roundhouse workspace needed to schedule, document, organize, or preserve work before the Owner joins.

A Temporary Admin may operate the unclaimed Entity within legitimate operational scope, including:

- building and preserving the Entity Timeline;
- using CAPTURE in the Entity context;
- creating Tasks / Lists and work records;
- scheduling the Business or Property where appropriate;
- associating legitimate work with Properties, Businesses, people, and jobs;
- maintaining the Entity's working record until ownership is established.

All Temporary Admin activity remains attributed to the person who actually performed it.

Temporary Admin authority does **not** create ownership and does not allow the Temporary Admin to represent themselves as the legitimate Owner.

When the legitimate Owner later claims the Entity, the same Entity remains. Its Timeline, photos, tasks, work history, records, and legitimate relationships are preserved.

## 3. Ownership and Authority

A Property or Business may have an **Owner or multiple Co-Owners**.

Ownership is separate from operational authority. An Owner or Co-Owner can give another person substantial authority without transferring ownership.

**Temporary Admin** is a temporary operational authority used only while ownership has not yet been established in Roundhouse or while an unclaimed Entity is awaiting transfer to its legitimate Owner.

Temporary Admin is not a base Role and does not by itself describe who the person is in relation to the Property or Business.

Once a legitimate Owner claim is completed, Temporary Admin authority ends unless the new Owner deliberately grants that person a continuing Role or management authority.

An Owner does not have to be the active Lead, and a Lead does not have to be an Owner.

When applicable, Roundhouse can show:

**Home:** Home Pro (Owner), Home Pro (Co-Owner), Home Pro (Lead), Homeowner, Homeowner (Co-Owner).

**Trade:** Trade Pro (Owner), Trade Pro (Co-Owner), Trade Pro (Lead), Trade Pro.

The same **Owner, Co-Owner, and Lead** distinctions apply where appropriate to Commercial and Supplier participation.

If none applies, no ownership or leadership designation is shown in parentheses.

## 4. Claims and Transfers

The legitimate Owner of a Property or Business can claim an Entity created by a Temporary Admin or another non-owner creator.

A normal claim may be completed by the current Temporary Admin approving transfer of control to the legitimate Owner.

If the Temporary Admin refuses, cannot be reached, or ownership is disputed, Roundhouse can require verification of the ownership claim. Appropriate evidence may include business registration, business license, ownership documents, Property records, or other reliable evidence appropriate to the Entity type.

A Temporary Admin does not gain the right to retain control of an Entity simply because they created or maintained it.

When a Property or Business changes ownership or an unclaimed Entity becomes claimed, **the existing Entity remains**. Ownership and current authority change; legitimate history does not reset.

The transfer changes who controls the Entity. It does not erase legitimate records created before the transfer.

## 5. Business Identity

A Business name is not by itself a unique identifier.

Different real-world Trade or Supplier Businesses may legitimately have the same or similar names.

A Business claim applies to the **specific real-world Business**, not exclusive use of that name throughout Roundhouse.

Business search results display identifying location information, such as:

**ABC Plumbing — Austin, TX**

to help identify the correct Business.

## 6. Records

Legitimate work becomes part of the Roundhouse historical record.

A record can legitimately appear in more than one place. Work performed at a Property can remain in that Property's history while also appearing in the history or portfolio of the Trade Professional, Trade Team Member, Supplier, or Business that contributed to it.

Once a photo or work record is uploaded, it becomes a **Roundhouse-hosted record**. The uploader cannot later remove the underlying record from Roundhouse.

Changing ownership, roles, employment, Business affiliation, access, Temporary Admin status, or claim status does not erase legitimate historical work.

## 7. Record Acceptance

A submitted work record can be:

**Accepted** — confirmed by someone with appropriate authority.

**Not Accepted** — submitted but not yet accepted or rejected.

**Rejected** — specifically declined.

**Disputed** — some part of the record or its association is challenged.

Creating an Entity or holding Temporary Admin authority does not automatically give someone authority to accept their own work on behalf of that Entity's legitimate Owner or authorized management.

## 8. Disputes

Either side can dispute a record, its accuracy, its association with an Entity, or an ownership claim.

A Residential Property owner can dispute work that did not occur at their Property. Commercial ownership or authorized Commercial Management can do the same for a Commercial Property.

A Trade or Supplier Business can dispute work or records incorrectly associated with its Business.

If someone creates an Entity using the identity of an existing real-world Property or Business, the legitimate Owner can claim the Entity and challenge records that do not belong to it.

If an ownership transfer is disputed, Roundhouse may require evidence sufficient to determine who legitimately controls the real-world Entity.

If a record challenge is established, those records can be **disassociated from the Entity without erasing the contributor's underlying historical record**.

The contributor or Temporary Admin does not retain the right to represent themselves through an Entity they do not legitimately control.

**Disassociation corrects where a record belongs. It does not erase the underlying historical record.**

## 9. Visibility

Record permanence and public visibility are separate.

If a photo is designated **publicly viewable**, Roundhouse reserves the right to display and showcase it within the Roundhouse platform.

Private photos are not used for public display.

Public work records should not automatically expose a private Property's street address. General location, such as ZIP Code, can be displayed instead.

## 10. Loss of Role or Access

Losing an active role does not erase a person's legitimate historical participation.

A former Home participant retains their own legitimate Home history through their permanent Identity and historical attribution. Where continued access to the Property itself is appropriate, an authorized Owner may give that person the **Viewer** Role, which provides only the visibility specifically granted and does not restore former Team authority.

A former Trade participant retains their own legitimate Trade history and portfolio through their permanent Identity and historical attribution without becoming a Trade Viewer and without retaining access to the former Business, Property, Job, or client information.

The same principle applies when someone leaves Commercial Management, a Commercial Team, or a Supplier Business: **their legitimate historical contribution remains even though their current authority ends.**

A former Temporary Admin likewise retains attribution for legitimate work and records they created, but ownership transfer or loss of Temporary Admin authority ends their operational control unless a new legitimate Role or permission source remains.

Historical visibility does not restore operational authority.

## Governing Rule

**Every new Roundhouse Entity is created in one of two capacities: Owner or Temporary Admin. Owner asserts legitimate ownership. Temporary Admin creates and maintains an Unclaimed Entity without asserting ownership until the legitimate Owner claims it.**

**A legitimate ownership claim can correct who controls an Entity and what legitimately belongs to that Entity without erasing the legitimate historical records of the people who created or contributed them.**

Changes in ownership, control, role, access, Temporary Admin status, acceptance, dispute status, or Entity association do not erase legitimate underlying historical records.
