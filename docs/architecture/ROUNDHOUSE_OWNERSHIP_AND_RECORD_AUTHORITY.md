# ROUNDHOUSE — OWNERSHIP & RECORD AUTHORITY

## Purpose

This document defines ownership, control, claims, record authority, disputes, and historical preservation for RoundHouse.

**Creation, ownership, control, access, and record authority are separate.**

Entity definitions are governed by **`ROUNDHOUSE_ENTITY_MODEL.md`**. Role titles, subscription requirements, creation limits, and operational authority are governed by **`ROUNDHOUSE_ROLES_AND_PERMISSIONS.md`**.

## 1. Properties and Businesses

There should be **one RoundHouse Entity for each real-world Property or Business**.

Owners and participants may change. The Property or Business and its legitimate history remain.

A person's private History displays their own legitimate participation as defined by the Entity model and **`../HISTORY_ENTITY_LOGIC.md`**.

## 2. Creating a Property or Business

RoundHouse searches for an existing Property or Business before another is created.

A person with a legitimate reason to interact with a Property or Business may create its RoundHouse record if it does not already exist, within the creation limits and available functions of their account.

Setup records whether the creator is asserting legitimate ownership or establishing a working record without asserting ownership. The applicable Role and authority are established through intake.

### Admin and unclaimed records

A Property or Business established without a legitimate Owner claim is marked **Unclaimed**.

The **Home Admin**, **Trade Admin**, and **Supplier Admin** roles require paid access. They allow the appropriate Property or Business to be administered before its legitimate Owner connects, without representing the Admin as its Owner.

Examples include a Trade participant establishing a subcontractor's Business to document work, an Admin setting up a Property where work is occurring, or an authorized administrator establishing the working record for a Commercial Property.

Admin operational functions follow Roles & Authority. All activity remains attributed to the person who actually performed it.

When the legitimate Owner later claims the Property or Business, the same record remains. Its Timeline, photos, tasks, Work history, records, and legitimate relationships are preserved.

## 3. Ownership and Authority

A Property or Business may have one legitimate Owner or multiple people who share ownership.

Ownership is separate from operational authority. An Owner can delegate management without transferring ownership. Shared ownership does not by itself determine who manages daily activity or performs professional work.

Role titles use the Owner, Manager, Lead, and Admin positions defined in Roles & Authority. Lead responsibility applies to Trade and Supplier participation.

A claim that establishes the legitimate Owner ends the non-owner creator's temporary administrative control unless the Owner deliberately authorizes a continuing relationship. Independently valid permission sources are evaluated separately.

## 4. Claims and Transfers

The legitimate Owner of a Property or Business can find its existing RoundHouse record and **request ownership transfer** from the person currently administering it.

A normal claim is completed by the current controller approving the legitimate Owner's request. A free account cannot proactively offer or initiate transfer of a Property or Business it created.

If the current controller refuses, cannot be reached, or ownership is disputed, RoundHouse can require verification of the ownership claim. Appropriate evidence may include business registration, business license, ownership documents, Property records, or other reliable evidence appropriate to the claim.

Creating or maintaining the record does not give someone the right to retain control against an established legitimate ownership claim.

When a Property or Business changes ownership or becomes claimed, **the existing Entity remains**. Ownership and current authority change; legitimate history does not reset.

History remains private to its associated person and is not transferred with a Property or Business.

## 5. Business Identity

A Business name is not by itself a unique identifier.

Different real-world Trade or Supplier Businesses may legitimately have the same or similar names.

A Business claim applies to the **specific real-world Business**, not exclusive use of that name throughout RoundHouse.

Business search results display identifying location information, such as:

**ABC Plumbing — Austin, TX**

to help identify the correct Business.

## 6. Records

Legitimate work becomes part of the RoundHouse historical record.

A record can legitimately appear in more than one place. Work performed at a Property can remain in that Property's history, remain associated with the Business that contributed to it, and appear in the participating person's private History.

These views preserve the same underlying record, source, and attribution.

Once a photo or work record is uploaded, it becomes a **RoundHouse-hosted record**. The uploader cannot later remove the underlying record from RoundHouse.

Changing ownership, Roles, employment, Business affiliation, access, Admin status, or claim status does not erase legitimate historical work.

## 7. Record Acceptance

A submitted work record can be:

**Accepted** — confirmed by someone with appropriate authority.

**Not Accepted** — submitted but not yet accepted or rejected.

**Rejected** — specifically declined.

**Disputed** — some part of the record or its association is challenged.

Creating a Property or Business or holding Admin authority does not automatically authorize someone to accept their own work on behalf of that Property or Business's legitimate Owner or authorized management.

## 8. Disputes

Either side can dispute a record, its accuracy, its association with a Property or Business, or an ownership claim.

A Property Owner or authorized Property Manager can dispute work that did not occur at that Residential or Commercial Property.

A Trade or Supplier Business can dispute work or records incorrectly associated with its Business.

If someone creates a record using the identity of an existing real-world Property or Business, the legitimate Owner can claim it and challenge records that do not belong to it.

If an ownership transfer is disputed, RoundHouse may require evidence sufficient to determine who legitimately controls the real-world Property or Business.

If a record challenge is established, those records can be **disassociated from the Property or Business without erasing the contributor's underlying historical record**. History displays the corrected association and retains legitimate authorship.

The contributor or Admin does not retain the right to represent themselves through a Property or Business they do not legitimately control.

**Disassociation corrects where a record belongs. It does not erase the underlying historical record.**

## 9. Visibility

Record permanence and public visibility are separate.

If a photo is designated **publicly viewable**, RoundHouse reserves the right to display and showcase it within the RoundHouse platform.

Private photos are not used for public display.

Public work records should not automatically expose a private Property's street address. General location, such as ZIP Code, can be displayed instead.

Public publishing remains subject to the current Role, authority, and subscription rules. Share RoundHouse invites someone to the platform; it does not publish private Property or Business records.

## 10. Loss of Role or Access

Losing an active Role does not erase a person's legitimate historical participation.

The person's contributions remain visible in their private History with the Role, authority, date, and Property or Business context under which they were created. Current access ends with its governing relationship; independently authorized access remains in effect.

Continued viewing of a specific Property requires a separate current Viewer authorization. Viewing personal contributions in History does not grant current access to the former Property, Business, Job, client information, or other participants' records.

A former Admin retains attribution for legitimate work and records they created. Their operational control ends when its authority ends unless another legitimate permission source remains.

**Historical visibility does not restore operational authority.**

## Governing Rule

**Legitimate ownership claims establish who controls a Property or Business. Role titles and operational authority follow Roles & Authority. Ownership, access, and Role changes preserve legitimate historical records and their original attribution. A person's History remains private to that person and does not restore access to former Properties or Businesses.**
